A few honest observations after <!-- X --> semesters of studying computer science.
Not a listicle. Just things that actually changed how I work.

## Theory is a tool, not a destination

For a while I treated data structures and algorithms like hurdles to clear,
not knowledge to internalize. That was wrong. When you understand *why* a hash map
works, you stop having to memorize — you derive.

## Read code, not just tutorials

Tutorials show you happy paths. Real codebases show you tradeoffs.
Pick an open-source project in a language you know and read it like a book.
You'll learn more in a week than from most courses.

## Start before you're ready

I kept waiting until I knew "enough" to build things. That moment doesn't arrive.
The knowledge you're waiting for only comes from building.

## Ask for help sooner than feels comfortable

Struggling productively for 20 minutes is good. Struggling silently for 4 hours isn't.
Most people in tech are happy to help. Let them.

---

These aren't universal. Your mileage may vary. But they've been useful to me.
